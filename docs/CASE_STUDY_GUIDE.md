# Case Study Writing Guide

## Purpose

Case studies should lead with business outcomes, not technical implementation. The reader should understand the value delivered within the first two paragraphs.

## Recommended Structure

### 1. Hook

One sentence framing the business problem. Write for a non-technical decision-maker.

### 2. Business Impact

Measurable results framed in business terms: revenue, costs saved, time recovered, users gained, conversion improvements. Use specific numbers.

### 3. What I Did

Plain-language explanation of the approach. Avoid jargon. Focus on decisions and methodology, not tools.

### 4. Challenge

The constraints, timeline pressure, or complexity that made this project difficult.

### 5. Technical Deep-Dive

Architecture, patterns, tools, and implementation details. This section is for technical readers and can use developer terminology freely.

### 6. Lessons Learned (Optional)

Reflections on what worked, what was surprising, or what you'd do differently.

## Tone Guidelines

- Lead with outcomes, not outputs
- Frame results as business value (costs, speed, revenue, users) not technical metrics
- A non-technical reader should grasp the value from the opening paragraphs
- Keep technical depth in dedicated sections, not scattered throughout
- Use concrete numbers whenever possible
